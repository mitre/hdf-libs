## Context

ADR-0008 to ADR-0011 give the platform a contract, a store, a data layer, and a service. Read as one, they omit what a hosted, multi-tenant compliance store needs on day one and what the standards it will be held to require. The round-6 gap analysis checked each candidate against the ADR text:

- **No audit trail.** `documents.created_by` (ADR-0009 §8), agent attribution inside amendments (ADR-0007 §3), redacted structured logs (ADR-0011), and ADR-0005's requirement-change events are all that exists. None records who imported, deleted, re-projected, embedded, created a client, rotated a key, changed a role, or failed to sign in. NIST SP 800-53 AU-2/AU-3/AU-12, AC-2(4), AC-6(9) and OWASP ASVS V16 name exactly these events. Logs are not the audit record: they rotate, they are mutable, and ADR-0011 redacts them by design.
- **No background execution.** ADR-0008 §3 declares "no `202` — no producer exists"; ADR-0010 §4's `Indexer.rebuild` and ADR-0009 §12's re-projection are synchronous calls. A schema bump over a large store or an embedding rebuild over thousands of documents does not fit in a request.
- **No organization lifecycle.** ADR-0009 §5 deletes one document with its cascade; ADR-0011 §3 creates organizations. Nothing deletes an organization (ADR-0009 deliberately has no FKs into auth), exports a tenant's data, or holds a document against deletion.
- **No delivery of events.** ADR-0005 defined the requirement-change-event stream and its kernel and stated that hdf-libs owns the schema and pure functions, not the producer or transport. Ingest into a store that knows the prior document for the same system is the producer ADR-0005 was waiting for.
- **No home for evidence bytes.** `Content_Reference.uri` lands in `document_refs.raw_ref` (ADR-0009 §3); `hdf_validate checksums` needs a `FetchFunc` (ADR-0007) the service has nothing to feed.
- **No quotas, no posture, no backup.** Rate limiting is per key and per minute; nothing bounds documents or bytes per organization; TLS, CORS, and proxy trust are named in ADR-0011 §1 but have no operational doc; PGlite file mode and PostgreSQL have no backup and restore procedure; `/metrics` is unmet.

Facts the design rests on (verified 2026-09-02 against the pinned tarballs and docs): **pg-boss** 12.x is a Postgres-backed job queue with no extension (its own `pgboss` schema, polling with optional `LISTEN`/`NOTIFY`) that ships a PGlite adapter — it keeps the single-store invariant on both engines; its `start()` creates the schema and runs migrations unless `createSchema: false` and `migrate: false` are set (then it throws on a version mismatch); "one active job per key" needs a queue created with `policy: 'singleton'` plus a `singletonKey`; retries are `retryLimit`/`retryBackoff`/`retryDelay` (defaults 2, off); completed jobs are removed after `deleteAfterSeconds` (default seven days); graphile-worker needs a `pg` wire connection and cannot run in-process on PGlite. better-auth's `organization` plugin exposes `beforeDeleteOrganization`/`afterDeleteOrganization` hooks and records no per-session second-factor completion (the session exists only after the challenge passes; `user.twoFactorEnabled` is the durable flag). PGlite exposes `dumpDataDir()`/`loadDataDir()`; PostgreSQL `pg_dump`/`pg_restore`. **Standard Webhooks** (`webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature: v1,<base64>`, signed content `${id}.${timestamp}.${body}`, `whsec_` secrets, ±5 min tolerance, a ten-attempt schedule over about 24 hours) is the shape the webhook ecosystem converged on. A table owner or superuser can always drop a trigger, so "append-only" is a property against the runtime role and application bugs, never against the owner — the tamper-evident copy has to live elsewhere or be hash-chained. The container image is ADR-0011 Phase 8's; the repo's supply-chain epic (`hdf-libs-wgvm`) covers CLI artifacts.

## Decision

### 0. Placement, roles, and the table class
- **Where routes live.** The companion document (ADR-0011 §2) is **strictly `/api/auth/*`** — the paths better-auth serves. Every HDF-owned route this ADR adds lives in ADR-0008's main document as a synthetic operation under ADR-0008 §2's rule: request and response shapes are conventions components or reflected types registered through prerequisite (b)'s hook (a TypeScript twin hook in `hdf-serve` registers the service's shapes), referenced by name only; hand-set class and scopes; one card per operation; the no-fragment AC applies. The routes, their routers in `hdf-serve`, and their scopes:

| Route | Router | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /audit` (`Accept: application/json` or `application/x-ndjson` in the ECS shape) | `audit` | `hdf:admin` |
| `202` on job-producing operations; `GET /jobs/{id}`; `GET /jobs/{id}/result` | `jobs` | as the producing operation; `hdf:admin` for export results |
| `PUT` / `GET /documents/{id}/evidence/{sha256}` | `documents` | `hdf:write` / `hdf:read` |
| `POST` / `DELETE /documents/{id}/hold` | `documents` | `hdf:admin` |
| `POST /organizations/{id}/export`; `GET /organizations/{id}/usage` | `organizations` | `hdf:admin` / `hdf:read` |
| `GET` / `POST` / `DELETE /webhooks`, `/webhooks/{id}` | `webhooks` | `hdf:admin` |

- **Roles.** The service runs as the non-owner runtime role (ADR-0011 §3). Everything that needs ownership runs in `hdf-serve migrate` as the migrations role: this ADR's ledger migrations, the `pgboss` schema installation, and the grants below. The entrypoint's ownership check excludes the `pgboss` schema, which pg-boss owns.
- **Tables.** This ADR **owns** the schema and migrations of `audit_events`, `jobs_meta`, `blobs`, `outbox`, `webhook_subscriptions`, `organization_quotas`, and the `documents.hold` column; they are **hosted in `hdf-db-schema`'s ledger** (ADR-0009 §9) under that package's build, sync, and ledger-parity rules, and they belong to ADR-0009 §8's **organization-anchored** class: tenancy columns directly on the row, no `documents` FK, the generated tenant predicate for reads, excluded from every cascade. `hold` is one of ADR-0009 §5's two operational columns.

### 1. Audit ledger — append-only against the runtime role, hash-chained, exportable
- **Table** `audit_events(id uuid, at timestamptz, actor_sub text, actor_kind ∈ {user, client, apikey, system}, organization_id uuid NULL, team_id uuid NULL, request_id text NULL, action text, subject_kind text, subject_id text, outcome ∈ {ok, denied, error}, code text NULL, details jsonb, prev_hash bytea NULL, row_hash bytea)`. `INSERT` is granted to the runtime role; a trigger rejects every `UPDATE` and every `DELETE` except one path: `DELETE` by the migrations role with `current_setting('hdf.audit_retention', true) = '1'` set inside the `SECURITY DEFINER` function `hdf_audit_retain(days)` (the ADR-0009 §11 pattern, `EXECUTE` granted to the runtime role), which removes rows older than `days` and inserts one `audit.retention` row summarising the count. **The honest boundary**: the ledger is append-only against the runtime role and against application bugs; the table owner and superusers can tamper. Tamper-evidence is therefore a **per-organization hash chain** — `row_hash = sha256(canonical row without hashes || prev_hash)`, `prev_hash` = the previous row's hash for the same organization (a separate chain for `organization_id IS NULL`), computed by the insert trigger — verified by `hdf-serve audit verify` and, as the tamper-evident **copy**, the ECS export to a SIEM (ADR-0002's shape; the platform's own audit joins the same pipeline as its assessment data).
- **Policies of its own** (it is organization-anchored, and auth events precede any organization): `INSERT` is unconditional for the runtime role (auth hooks write outside the tenant transaction); `SELECT` is `organization_id IS NOT DISTINCT FROM <ctx> OR (organization_id IS NULL AND actor_sub = <ctx.subject>)`, so an organization's admin sees its rows and a user sees their own organization-less events (failed sign-ins); rows with `actor_kind = system` and no organization (bootstrap, retention) are readable only by a local principal (`hdf db audit`) and the export.
- **Writers**: ADR-0010's `AuditSink` implementation writes one row inside the mutating transaction for `put`, `delete`, `reproject`, `rebuild`, `buildIndex`; ADR-0011 writes auth events through `databaseHooks` and its own routes — sign-in success/failure, lockout, second-factor changes, invitation sent/accepted, role change, organization create/delete, OAuth client and API key create/revoke, token exchange, every `403`, and every administrative route; this ADR's own services write for jobs, exports, holds, webhooks (each delivery attempt is an `webhook.delivery` row, which is why there is no deliveries table), and quota rejections. The action vocabulary is one tracked file (`src/audit/actions.json`); the **totality test** enumerates the app's non-`GET` routes plus the `DocumentStore`/`Indexer` mutating method names, asserts each has an entry, and asserts each entry has at least one writer test. `details` is a **closed Zod schema** (`src/audit/details.ts`: `reason?: code`, `ip?: string`, `userAgentHash?: string`, `counts?: Record<string, number>`, `ids?: uuid[]`) — no free-form keys, never document content; the ADR-0009 canary runs over this table too. `request_id` is the same id the `500` problem body carries (ADR-0008 §3) and the `Principal.requestId` of ADR-0010 §2.
- **Read surface**: `GET /audit` (§0), cursor-paginated, filterable by `actor`, `action`, `subject`, time window; `hdf db audit` in the CLI; `hdf-serve audit verify` walks the chains and reports the first break.

### 2. Job runner — pg-boss on the shared handle, non-owner at runtime
- **Installation and roles.** `hdf-serve migrate` (owner) constructs a throwaway `PgBoss({migrate: true})` to install or upgrade the `pgboss` schema, then grants `USAGE` on the schema and `SELECT`/`INSERT`/`UPDATE`/`DELETE` on `pgboss.*` to the runtime role. The service constructs `PgBoss({migrate: false, createSchema: false})` on the shared handle and fails fast on a version mismatch. `HDF_JOBS_WORKERS` (default 2), `HDF_JOBS_POLL_MS` (default 2000). Runs on PGlite in dev mode and PostgreSQL in production.
- **Principal snapshot.** Job payloads carry **ids and a principal snapshot only** — `{subject, organizationId, teamId, scopes, jti, requestId}` — never document content. At job start the worker **re-validates** the snapshot: the `jti` is not in the denylist, the subject is still a member of the organization with a role whose scopes ⊇ the snapshot's (builtin mode; external mode skips the membership check, documented), else the job is `failed` with `FORBIDDEN_SCOPE` and an audit event. The worker then re-opens documents through `hdf-data` under that principal, so tenancy and audit apply to background work exactly as to requests.
- **Tenant-scoped job ids.** pg-boss ids carry no organization, so `jobs_meta(job_id uuid PK, organization_id, team_id, kind, subject_id, created_by, created_at)` (organization-anchored) is written with every enqueue; `GET /jobs/{id}` resolves through it and returns the uniform 404 otherwise. Completed jobs disappear from pg-boss after `deleteAfterSeconds` (seven days); `jobs_meta` keeps the terminal state and result reference so `/jobs/{id}` answers until `HDF_AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS` removes it.
- **Jobs in v1**: `embeddings.rebuild(documentId)` and `embeddings.buildIndex(modelId)` (ADR-0010 §4), `schema.reproject(documentId)` fanned out by `schema.reprojectAll(fromVersion)` (ADR-0009 §12), `org.export(organizationId)` (§3), `webhooks.deliver(outboxId)` (§5), `audit.retention`, `blobs.gc` (§4). Each `kind` is a queue created with `policy: 'singleton'`; the `singletonKey` is `<organization>:<subject id>`, so a duplicate request while a job is active returns the existing job (`200` with its body rather than a new `202`). Retries: `retryLimit: 3`, `retryBackoff: true`; a job that exhausts retries is `failed` with an ADR-0010 §6 code and an audit event.
- **Contract** (ADR-0008 §3): a job-producing operation returns `202` with `Location: /jobs/{id}`, `Retry-After`, and the job body; `GET /jobs/{id}` returns `200` with `Retry-After` while `state ∈ {created, active}` and, on `completed`, `result: {ref}` (a relative URL) — no `303`; `{id, kind, state ∈ {created, active, completed, failed, cancelled}, createdAt, startedAt?, completedAt?, result?, problem?}`. `hdf db jobs` mirrors it.
- **Shutdown**: ADR-0011 §1's graceful shutdown calls pg-boss `stop({graceful: true})` inside its bound; no separate drain command.

### 3. Organization lifecycle — delete guard, tenant export, legal hold
- **Delete**: better-auth's `beforeDeleteOrganization` hook refuses deletion while `documents` rows exist for the organization unless the request carries `cascade: true` **and** the caller's token is a user token (`actor_kind = user`) with role owner and `amr ∋ mfa` — the claim ADR-0011 §3 derives from `user.twoFactorEnabled`; client and API-key tokens are refused. A cascading delete is a job that deletes every document through `hdf-data.delete` (audit row per document, holds respected — a held document fails the job), then lets better-auth remove members, invitations, clients, and keys; `afterDeleteOrganization` writes the final audit event. Nothing crosses from `hdf-db-schema` into auth tables by FK — the job is the only bridge.
- **Export** (`POST /organizations/{id}/export`, job): a tar of every retained document's original bytes plus a manifest (`documents.json` with ids, sha256, docType, `validated_against`, timestamps, and the ids of non-retained documents; `refs.json`), stored as a `blobs` row with `kind = 'export'` (exempt from the per-blob cap, bounded by `HDF_EXPORT_MAX_BYTES`, default 2 GB), downloadable from `GET /jobs/{id}/result` (`200`, `application/x-tar`, `Content-Disposition: attachment`, `Content-Length`, `Accept-Ranges: bytes` with `206`/`416`, `410` after `HDF_EXPORT_TTL_HOURS`, default 24) — every download is an `org.export.download` audit row. Cross-tenant content is excluded by construction: `document_refs` resolve in-tenant (ADR-0009 §3). Audit rows are exported through `GET /audit`, not bundled.
- **Legal hold**: `documents.hold` (`boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false`, an ADR-0009 §5 operational column) set and cleared by `hdf:admin` through `POST`/`DELETE /documents/{id}/hold`, both audited; `delete` and cascading organization delete refuse a held document with `HELD` (ADR-0010 §6). A two-person rule for clearing is deferred and named as a follow-on; holds apply to documents only, never to `audit_events`, whose retention runs regardless. Archival tiers are out of scope.

### 4. Evidence bytes and quotas
- **`BlobStore`** interface `{ put(bytes, {principal, sha256, mediaType}) → {sha256, size, created}, get(sha256, principal) → {bytes, mediaType}, head }` with one v1 implementation: a `blobs(organization_id, team_id, sha256 bytea, kind ∈ {evidence, export}, size bigint, media_type text, bytes bytea, created_at, PRIMARY KEY (organization_id, team_id, sha256) NULLS NOT DISTINCT)` table (organization-anchored; no cross-tenant deduplication, so identical bytes in two organizations are two rows and never an existence oracle; `HDF_BLOB_MAX_BYTES`, default 64 MB, for `kind = 'evidence'`), and an interface seam for an object-storage implementation later (Alternative D). Codes: a missing blob is `BLOB_NOT_FOUND` (404), an over-cap upload `TOO_LARGE` (413) — both in ADR-0010 §6.
- **Routes** (§0): `PUT /documents/{id}/evidence/{sha256}` requires `Content-Length` (`411` otherwise), `Content-Type` (stored as `media_type`, never sniffed), and `Repr-Digest: sha-256=:<base64>:` (RFC 9530) equal to the hex `{sha256}` in the path — the server recomputes and refuses a mismatch with `400`; an existing identical blob returns `200`, a new one `201`; the body is ADR-0008's `Binary` conventions component. `GET` returns `ETag: "<sha256>"`, `Content-Digest`, `Content-Disposition: attachment`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, and a `Content-Type` from an allowlist (`application/json`, `application/xml`, `text/plain`, `application/pdf`, `application/octet-stream`; anything else is served as `application/octet-stream`; never `text/html` or `image/svg+xml`). Blobs bind to the document's `Content_Reference` entries by `sha256`; `hdf_validate checksums` and ADR-0007's `FetchFunc` resolve `document_refs.raw_ref` of the form `sha256:<hex>` against the blob store first.
- **Garbage collection**: `blobs.gc` removes an evidence blob when no `document_refs` row of the same organization carries `raw_ref = 'sha256:<hex>'` for it **and** it is older than `HDF_BLOB_GRACE_HOURS` (default 24, so a blob uploaded before its document is ingested survives), and export bundles past their TTL.
- **Quotas** per organization: `organization_quotas(organization_id PK, max_documents, max_bytes)` (organization-anchored; a row is created with every organization, defaulting to `HDF_QUOTA_DOCUMENTS`/`HDF_QUOTA_BYTES`, default `0` = unlimited; editable by `hdf:admin`). Every quota-checked write (`put`, evidence `put`) takes `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on that row inside its mutating transaction, so concurrent writes cannot both pass, and exceeds with `QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (429 with a `quota` problem member, ADR-0010 §6) and an audit event. `GET /organizations/{id}/usage` returns documents, source bytes, blob bytes, and the limits.

### 5. Outbox and Standard Webhooks
- **Transactional outbox** `outbox(id, at, organization_id, event ∈ {document.ingested, document.deleted, document.reprojected, amendment.applied, job.completed, job.failed, organization.exported}, subject_id, payload jsonb, delivered_at NULL)` written in the same transaction as the mutation. **Payload rule**: ids, `docType`, `sha256`, counts, and timestamps only — never requirement ids, titles, or justifications (for `amendment.applied`: the amendment document id and the count). A pg-boss job delivers each row to every active subscription of **the same organization** (the delivery query joins on `s.organization_id = o.organization_id`).
- **Subscriptions** `webhook_subscriptions(id, organization_id, url, events[], secret_enc bytea, secret_next_enc bytea NULL, active)` managed through `/webhooks` (§0). The secret is a Standard Webhooks `whsec_` value, shown once at creation, stored encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from `HDF_AUTH_SECRET` (HKDF, info `hdf-webhook-secret`), and rotated through `secret_next` with a dual-signing window.
- **Delivery** follows **Standard Webhooks** verbatim: headers `webhook-id` (the outbox id), `webhook-timestamp` (unix seconds), `webhook-signature: v1,<base64 HMAC-SHA256>` over `${id}.${timestamp}.${body}`; the body is `{type, timestamp, data}`; receivers apply a ±5 minute tolerance and constant-time comparison. The target URL is validated at creation **and** at delivery time with ADR-0011 §3's guarded fetch (resolve once, reject special-use and private addresses, pin the resolved address, no redirects, HTTPS only). The retry schedule is the Standard Webhooks one (ten attempts over about 24 hours); after the last attempt the subscription's failure is an audit event and, after `HDF_WEBHOOK_MAX_FAILURES` (default 50) consecutive failures, the subscription is disabled. Every attempt is a `webhook.delivery` audit row; there is no deliveries table.
- **The ADR-0005 producer, named not built**: ingest of a results document whose system link (ADR-0009 §3) resolves a prior results document can derive `Requirement_Change_Event`s with the kernel that already exists in both languages (`ChangeEventFromPrevious` in `hdf-diff/go`, `changeEventFromPrevious` in `hdf-diff/src`) and emit them as `requirement.changed` outbox events. This ADR reserves the event name and the outbox row shape; the derivation itself is an **ADR-0005 amendment** carded after ADR-0009 Phase 7, because it decides how change events are persisted.

### 6. Deployment posture, metrics, backup
- **Posture doc** (`site/docs/guides/hdf-serve-deploy.md`): TLS terminates at a reverse proxy; the npm `start` binds loopback while the **container entrypoint defaults `HDF_BIND` to `0.0.0.0:8080`** (a proxy outside the container must reach it) and trusts only `HDF_TRUSTED_PROXIES`; `HDF_CORS_ORIGINS` is the single origin list; `HDF_DB_SSL=verify-full` is the container default; secrets by `_FILE`; the container's role checks; the rotation schedule for `HDF_AUTH_SECRET` and JWKS; what moves to the IdP in `external` mode. A compose example and a minimal Kubernetes manifest (no Helm chart in v1). Each checklist item names the test or boot-time assertion that evidences it.
- **Metrics**: Prometheus text exposition at `HDF_METRICS_BIND` (default `127.0.0.1:9464`; in the container `0.0.0.0:9464` on a port the compose example does not publish), unauthenticated, a separate listener: request count and latency by route and status, in-flight requests, rate-limit rejections, auth failures and lockouts, job queue depth and oldest-job age by kind, embeddings-provider latency and errors, database pool usage, total documents and blob bytes. **No per-organization label series** (unbounded cardinality, and organization ids would reach every scraper); per-organization numbers are the `usage` endpoint's. OpenTelemetry tracing stays deferred (ADR-0011 §7).
- **Backup and restore**: a documented procedure and a **CI-executed restore test** — PostgreSQL via `pg_dump --format=custom` of both ledgers' schemas plus `pgboss`, restored into a fresh container and verified by re-running the contract suite's read path; PGlite file mode via `dumpDataDir()` to a tarball and `loadDataDir()` into a fresh instance, verified the same way. The doc states that dumps contain `source_bytes`, `blobs.bytes`, and better-auth's password and second-factor hashes, so backup encryption is the deployer's (a checklist item). An ops note covers `REINDEX INDEX CONCURRENTLY` for HNSW indexes after mass deletion and `maintenance_work_mem` for large index builds (ADR-0009 §11).

## Reviewer challenge points

Will's review — and any agent review he runs — should specifically challenge or verify: (a) the **audit ledger** as an organization-anchored table in `hdf-db-schema`'s ledger with its own policies, the honest append-only boundary, and the per-organization hash chain plus SIEM export as tamper-evidence; (b) **pg-boss in-process** on the shared handle with the owner-installs / runtime-uses split and singleton queues, rather than an external worker or no jobs in v1; (c) **organization deletion as a job** that bridges the store and the auth tables without any FK, with cascade behind an MFA-bearing owner user token; (d) the **`bytea` blob store** keyed per tenant in v1 with an interface seam, rather than object storage now or no evidence bytes at all; (e) **Standard Webhooks in v1** with encrypted secrets and delivery-time SSRF pinning, and naming, not building, the ADR-0005 producer; (f) the **quota model** (a locked row per organization, `429` with a `quota` member) rather than per-route quotas; (g) a **separate loopback metrics listener without per-organization series** rather than an authenticated `/metrics` on the API port; (h) the **route placement rule** (§0) — everything HDF-owned in the main document, the companion strictly `/api/auth/*`; (i) the **container defaults** (`0.0.0.0` bind, `verify-full`) versus the npm defaults.

## Alternatives Considered

### A. Logs as the audit record
Pros: nothing to build. Cons: logs rotate, are mutable, are redacted by design, and are not queryable per tenant; ASVS 16.4.2 requires tamper-evidence. Why rejected: a store of evidence needs evidence of its handling — challenge (a).

### B. graphile-worker, BullMQ, or an external queue
Pros: mature workers. Cons: graphile-worker needs a `pg` wire connection (no PGlite in-process); BullMQ needs Redis — a second store, breaking invariant 3; an external queue is a second deployable. Why rejected: pg-boss keeps one store on both engines — challenge (b).

### C. No jobs in v1; everything synchronous
Pros: simplest. Cons: a schema-bump re-projection or an embedding rebuild over a real store cannot run in a request; organization export and webhook delivery need retries and durability. Why rejected: the reserved `202` pair in ADR-0008 exists for this — challenge (b).

### D. Object storage for evidence bytes now
Pros: right for large evidence. Cons: a second store and credential set in v1 for a feature with no measured demand. Why rejected for v1: `bytea` behind the `BlobStore` seam, object storage when a deployment needs it — challenge (d).

### E. Organization deletion by database cascade into auth tables
Pros: one statement. Cons: ADR-0009 §8 keeps the store free of FKs into auth; better-auth owns its tables and their deletion order. Why rejected — challenge (c).

### F. Metrics on the API port behind `hdf:admin`
Pros: one listener. Cons: scrapers do not hold API tokens, and a separate loopback listener is the community default. Why rejected — challenge (g).

### G. Custom `X-HDF-*` webhook headers (revision 1)
Pros: none over the standard. Cons: receivers have libraries for Standard Webhooks and none for a custom scheme. Why rejected in revision 2 — challenge (e).

### H. Audit rows as a cross-tenant global chain
Pros: one chain to verify. Cons: an organization's export would carry other tenants' hashes, and RLS-scoped reads could not verify continuity. Why rejected: per-organization chains — challenge (a).

## Consequences

Easier: every action on the platform has a tenant-scoped, hash-chained record with a SIEM path; long work leaves requests; organizations can be exported and deleted safely; evidence bytes have a home; downstreams get Standard Webhooks; operators get metrics and a tested restore.

Harder: one more schema (`pgboss`) and six more tables in the ledger; a worker inside the service process to size; the owner-installs / runtime-uses split for pg-boss; export bundles and blobs need garbage collection; two more synthetic operation groups in ADR-0008's document; the audit action vocabulary must stay total as routes are added; the hash chain adds an insert-time cost.

Risks and mitigations: audit volume → the retention function and indexes on `(organization_id, at)`; pg-boss on PGlite misbehaving → Phase 1's first AC runs the runner on both engines, with the fallback of a PostgreSQL-only runner and synchronous execution in dev mode; a job running with a stale principal → re-validation at start; webhook targets as SSRF → delivery-time resolve-and-pin; blobs growing the database → per-blob cap, quotas, gc, and the object-storage seam; export bundles leaking → tenant-scoped, audited on download, TTL; the chain being read as proof against the owner → the doc states the boundary and the export is the copy.

## Implementation Plan

### Scope
IN: the six organization-anchored tables and the `hold` column (as ADR-0009 ledger migrations, owned here); `hdf_audit_retain`; the hash chain and `audit verify`; the `AuditSink` implementation and the action vocabulary with its totality test; the pg-boss installation split, runner, snapshot re-validation, and the seven job kinds; the `202`/`/jobs` contract and the audit, evidence, hold, export, usage, and webhook synthetic operations in ADR-0008's document; organization delete guard/cascade, export, hold; the `BlobStore` and quotas; outbox and Standard Webhooks delivery; the posture doc, metrics listener, backup/restore procedure with its CI restore test; the environment table.
OUT: the ADR-0005 producer (named, carded after ADR-0009 Phase 7); object storage; archival tiers; a two-person rule for holds; OpenTelemetry tracing; a Helm chart; per-route quotas; viewer-shaped queries (follow-on ADR).
Delivery: **one epic on one branch**, PR-sized by phase, sequenced behind ADR-0009 Phase 1a, ADR-0010 Phases 2 and 5, and ADR-0011 Phases 2–4 as listed. The ADR-0012 overview lands with the design PR.

### Quality Standards (inherited by every card)
- Every mutation on every surface leaves exactly one audit event, in the same transaction; `UPDATE` on `audit_events` is rejected for every role and `DELETE` for every path but `hdf_audit_retain` (planted); every chain verifies after every test suite.
- Jobs run under a re-validated principal snapshot; every job's store calls pass the tenant negatives; job ids resolve only in-tenant.
- No document content in `audit_events.details`, `outbox.payload`, job payloads, or metrics labels (canary reused).
- Both engines for the runner and the tables (PGlite in dev mode, PostgreSQL in the ubuntu job); the restore test executes in CI.
- TDD, >90% coverage, zero lint warnings, repo gauntlet; no commercial downstream consumers referenced anywhere.

### Verification Strategy
The audit totality test (every mutation and auth event → exactly one row, both engines) and the chain verification are the spine; each phase closes only when its tables are append-only or tenant-scoped as stated, its jobs run under re-validated snapshots that fail the tenant negatives when wrong, and its outbound calls refuse private addresses. Live test before close: on the compose stack, ingest a document, rebuild its embeddings as a job, subscribe a local webhook and receive a Standard Webhooks delivery, place a hold and see the delete refused, export the organization and download the bundle, restore the backup into a fresh container, and read every step back from `GET /audit` and `hdf-serve audit verify` — outputs pasted in card notes.
